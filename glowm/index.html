GLOWM

A clearer record of care. Exploring a better workflow for midwives.

  • UX Research
  • Platform Comparison
  • Mobile App
Duration
Nov 2024–Jun 2026
My work
Literature research & platform comparison
Project with
Professor Dr. Michelle Rogers

Problem

Record keeping needed a clearer flow, from finding a patient to recording care.

GLOWM brings clinical guidance and record keeping into a mobile app. Our focus was the record-keeping experience and how it could better fit the flow of a visit. Finding a patient, reviewing earlier visits, and recording care should take less effort.

Approach

My part in the research.

I partnered with my professor on the literature review and platform comparison. I organized the papers by topic, brought together recurring concerns, and identified areas of GLOWM to explore further.

  1. Review the literature.We reviewed studies on medical records, maternity care, mobile tools, and birth outcomes. This research guided our review of patient lookup, care history, and visit forms in GLOWM.
  2. Compare record workflows.We compared how GLOWM and open-source medical record systems organize patient information, visits, and data entry.
  3. Turn gaps into suggestions.We used the literature and platform findings to suggest clearer ways to find patient records, review previous care, and record visits.

This was literature research and platform comparison. We did not interview or observe midwives.

Open-source record systems

We reviewed two open-source platforms for managing patient records: OpenEMR and OpenMRS.

OpenEMR ↗
An electronic health record system that brings patient information, clinical visits, and forms together.
OpenMRS ↗
A flexible medical record platform with customizable records and forms for different care needs.

We chose them to compare how records, visits, and data entry are organized, then identify ideas that could fit midwives’ work on a small phone screen.

What the reading added.

Scantlebury and colleagues described extra work when paper and electronic records ran alongside each other. Helou and colleagues showed that midwives and doctors can have different needs from the same record system.

These studies helped us ask whether information was easy to find and whether entering it fitted the work. The birth-outcome papers gave wider context for supporting midwives.

What we found in GLOWM.

The review pointed to three parts of the record-keeping experience that needed closer attention.

Finding a patient’s record requires a separate paper list.

  • GLOWM lists patient IDs and dates of birth, rather than names.
  • A separate handwritten list connects each patient’s name to their ID.
  • The midwife uses that list to identify which record to open.
  • The app’s patient list has no visible search or visit status.

Earlier care sits inside separate record sections.

  • Midwives may need to review a patient’s earlier care before continuing the current visit.
  • When a record opens, GLOWM shows a menu of sections, with pregnancy visits listed by number.
  • Recent readings and visit dates aren’t shown together on the record’s first screen. Midwives need to open separate sections to review previous care.

Recording a visit means working through a long form.

  • During a pregnancy visit, midwives record measurements, checks, and notes.
  • GLOWM combines these in one long form, using number fields, sliders, and yes/no options.
  • Some field labels appear more than once.
  • We wanted to explore a clearer form that helps midwives enter exact readings.

Solution & impact

Three ideas to test.

We identified three workflow gaps and recommendations for GLOWM. I connected the literature and platform review to these priorities. User testing has not started.

  1. 1
    Find the right record.Add patient-ID search and visit details, and review how to confirm identity while protecting privacy.
  2. 2
    Review care at a glance.Bring key details, dated visits, and previous readings together, with unfinished work clearly marked.
  3. 3
    Make visit entry clearer.Use short form sections, consistent number fields with units, and clear draft or completed status.

Future work.

We are waiting for Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval to work with nurses at Drexel and other campuses. After approval, we plan to:

  1. Test the current app.Ask nurses to use GLOWM and share what feels clear, where they get stuck, and what could be easier.
  2. Redesign with their feedback.Use their feedback to improve navigation, record organization, and data entry.
  3. Test the redesign again.Ask nurses to try the redesign and check whether tasks are clearer and easier to complete.

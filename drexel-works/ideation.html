Ideation

Understanding what drove me to solve this problem

Why I chose one place for campus jobs

Through the survey and interviews I conducted, I found that students were putting more effort into finding reliable job information than they should have to, while balancing their job search with their studies. This added burden motivated me to explore a simpler experience.

ThemeIssue identifiedWhat students needed
Scattered job search Students check several sources and struggle to follow updates while managing their studies. Bring listings and application updates into one place.
Unclear eligibility Students cannot easily tell which roles accept F-1, work-study, or domestic students. Clearly show who can apply and who receives priority.
Missing job details Hours, requirements, and qualifications are not always explained. Present consistent details so students can judge whether a role fits.
Overlooked opportunities Familiar jobs, such as dining hall roles, get attention while other department openings go unnoticed. Help students discover roles across campus departments.

Brain Dump

I started by writing down possible solutions for each problem without judging them immediately.

Finding jobs

  • Show all campus jobs in one place.
  • Send a weekly email with new jobs.
  • Show job locations on a campus map.
  • Let students browse jobs by department.
  • Suggest jobs based on students interests.
  • Send alerts when matching jobs appear.

Eligibility

  • Help students check whether they can apply.
  • Label who can apply for each job.
  • Explain which students get hiring priority.
  • Explain what work-study means.
  • Let students ask employers about eligibility.
  • Filter jobs by eligibility.

Job details

  • Show the pay and weekly hours.
  • Let students compare saved jobs.
  • Show the available work shifts.
  • List the skills each job requires.
  • Show application deadlines and start dates.
  • Show when each listing was last updated.

Applications

  • Let students save jobs for later.
  • Fill in details from their student profile.
  • Let students reuse or replace their résumé.
  • List what students need before applying.
  • Show the status of each application.
  • Remind students before applications close.

Worst Possible Idea

I explored ideas that would make the student job search more frustrating. This helped me question assumptions, identify what to avoid, and find opportunities to create a simpler experience.

  • Keep expired jobs visible.
  • Hide pay and working hours.
  • Reveal eligibility after applying.
  • Require an account for every department.
  • Send every job alert to everyone.
  • Make students enter the same details again.
  • Make students visit departments to find openings.
  • Remove search and department filters.
  • Show only dining hall jobs.
  • Leave out the skills each job requires.
  • Give no confirmation after applying.
  • Make students contact employers for every status update.

Narrowing down the ideas

I combined related ideas and focused the first version on helping students find relevant jobs, understand the requirements, and manage their applications. Features such as a campus map and job comparisons could come later. This focused direction became Drexel Works.

Bringing the ideas together

These ideas came together as Drexel Works: one place for students to find campus jobs, check whether they can apply, and follow their applications.

  1. Find a job
  2. Check the details
  3. Apply
  4. Track progress

Turning the idea into screens

The next step was to decide what students would see on each screen and how they would move from finding a job to applying. The focus was on keeping job details easy to find and the steps easy to follow.

Brandroom

Designing one home for a small brand

Helping designers build a small business's brand, then keep every file and post true to it.

RoleUI/UX designer
ToolsFigma · Claude

Brandroom is a free web app I designed for freelance designers who do branding for small businesses. They can build a color palette, keep the client's logos and templates in one place, and check that a post or reel is easy to read and on-brand before it goes live.

Problem

Small business owners need a brand, but they can't build one alone.

They don't have design training, and hiring help feels expensive and like a lot of work. So the designer they hire ends up doing everything, often across scattered files and tools.

Approach

What I heard, and what I saw as a designer.

I spoke with two small business owners. Both wanted their brand to look more polished and consistent, and that work lands on the designer they hire.

As a designer, I know what that looks like: client files spread across folders, and color palettes that need fixing before anything looks right. AI tools can make a post in seconds, but the designs often look generic and have no real taste. Designers need one place to organize a client's brand, fix its color palette and run readability checks easily.

My first version felt like several apps.

My first version gave each job its own screen: one for building a palette, one for checking contrast, one for the brand guide. It worked, but moving between them felt like using several apps.

I used Claude to help me ideate and research, and to run accessibility and readability checks. I designed Brandroom myself.

Solution

One place for a designer to build a client's brand and keep it consistent.

Brandroom helps designers create a color palette, check that designs are readable and accessible, set simple brand guidelines and store the client's files together.

  1. 1

    Brand library

    Each client's logos, fonts, templates and mood boards sit in one place, and it flags old logos still used in templates.

  2. 2

    Fix my colors

    Brandroom suggests fixes for a palette, like making a color darker so text is easier to read. The designer applies or skips each one, so nothing changes without their say.

  3. 3

    Readability check

    Every color pair is tested with the WCAG contrast rule and marked "Easy to read", "Big text only" or "Hard to read", so designers know where each pair is safe to use.

  4. 4

    Brand check

    Upload a post or reel from Canva and get Readable and On-brand scores, with exact fixes to copy back into Canva.

The biggest improvement

Brandroom brings palette fixes, readability checks and file storage into a single free app, so designers stop jumping between tools.

What I learned

Designing for the designer, not the owner, made every decision clearer. Next, I'll test it with designers to see where it helps most.
